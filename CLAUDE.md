## Commands

```sh
go run ./cmd/wg -h                                       # the CLI: every stage option is a flag
go run ./cmd/wg -width 2000 -height 2000 -rounds 10000 -ocean 52 -render all -out var -name 2k
go run ./cmd/wg -land-cells 10000 -ocean 52 -render biomes -out var   # size the map from a land-cell target
go build ./...
go vet ./...
go test ./...
go test ./voronoi -run TestNeighborsAreSymmetric   # one test

# write PNGs for visual inspection into var/ (gitignored; paths are relative to the package dir)
go test ./fracture -run 'TestGenerate$' -render=../var   # var/fracture-topo.png
go test ./voronoi -run TestRender -render=../var         # var/voronoi-mesh.png
go test ./terrain -run TestRender -render=../var         # var/terrain.png
go test ./rivers -run TestRender -render=../var          # var/rivers.png, var/rivers-mesh.png (with cell borders)
go test ./moisture -run TestRender -render=../var        # var/moisture.png
go test ./biomes -run TestRender -render=../var          # var/biomes.png, var/biomes-mesh.png
```

The `-render` flag is defined in the `fracture`, `voronoi`, `terrain`, `rivers`, `moisture` and `biomes` test files. Every `TestRender` except `fracture`'s skips without it.

## Architecture

A fantasy map generator built as a pipeline of stages. Each stage is a package with an `Options` struct, a `DefaultOptions()` (the values the tests use), and a `Generate` that is deterministic for the same inputs.

The `voronoi` stage and the stages after it are inspired by Amit Patel's [Polygonal Map Generation for Games](http://www-cs-students.stanford.edu/~amitp/game-programming/polygon-map-generation/). Consult it when designing later stages (elevation, moisture, rivers, biomes on the mesh).

1. **`fracture`**: makes the height map. Each round raises or lowers every pixel inside a random circle by 1, and the result is normalized to 0…1. It is adapted from `pkg/generators/flat` in github.com/mdhender/mapgen; `TestMatchesReference` checks it pixel for pixel against the original's per-pixel circle test. The defaults are 1920×1080 pixels, 1,000 rounds and seed `0x0123456789abcdef`. Elevations come in whole steps (about 58 distinct levels at the defaults), and later stages and renderers have to allow for that.
2. **`voronoi`**: lays a mesh over a `*heightmap.Map`. It triangulates random sites with `github.com/fogleman/delaunay`, builds each cell by clipping the map rectangle against the bisectors of its Delaunay neighbors (edges are labeled, so neighbors come from the clipped polygon), and runs a fixed number of Lloyd relaxation passes. `connect` then builds the article's second graph: polygon points within `mergeDistance` (1e-6) of each other become one `Corner` (neighboring cells compute shared corners separately, and cocircular sites leave near-zero edges), each polygon side becomes an `Edge` shared by its two cells (`Cells[1]` is −1 on the map border), and each cell's `Polygon` is rewritten from the merged corner points, so neighbors derive from the merged graph. A cell's elevation is the mean of the pixels whose centers lie inside it. Ocean is the lowest `OceanPercent` of cells *by count*, and `Mesh.SeaLevel` is −1 when there is no ocean. The only user-facing options are `CellSize` and `OceanPercent`, plus the seed that every random stage must take. `CellSize` (default 14) is the mean cell width in pixels; `Options.CellCount(width, height)` gives the resulting count, `width·height/CellSize²`. Cells are sized in pixels because a cell's elevation averages the pixels inside it: cells that are too small are noisy with fracture steps, and cells that are too large blur the coastline.
3. **`terrain`**: annotates a `*voronoi.Mesh` (article §3–4) in arrays indexed like the mesh's cells and corners, leaving the mesh untouched. Elevation comes from the height map, not distance to the coast as in the article. A corner's elevation is the mean of its cells. Every water cell is ocean. Outlets are corners that touch an ocean cell or lie on the map border, so land may run off the map and drain there. A priority-flood (Barnes et al. 2014) from the outlets raises pits by `epsilon` steps so every corner has a strictly lower `Downslope`. A pit at least `LakeDepth` deep that encloses a whole cell becomes a flat `Lake` at its spill level, with one `Outlet`. Shallower pits are only filled. It takes no seed, because nothing in it is random.
4. **`rivers`**: rains on the land and passes the water down the terrain's downslopes in upstream-first order. Corners are processed once nothing still flows into them. Water is measured in square pixels of land drained: each land cell's `Area()` is shared equally among its corners. Thresholds therefore don't depend on cell count. The default test map is small, so tune options by area, not by counts of cells or corners. `TestDensity` checks that 14 px and 7 px cells give similar rivers. A downslope edge carrying at least `MinFlow` becomes a river (`River[edge]` = flow), except between two corners of the same lake. This departs from article §5, which starts rivers at random mountain corners: rain everywhere lets the height map place them, and it needs no seed. The article computes moisture after rivers, from the distance to fresh water.
5. **`moisture`** (article §6, following mapgen2's `Map.as`): sources are lake corners (strength 1), river corners (strength `Flow/MinFlow`, capped at 3), and coast corners (strength `SeaStrength`, default 2, which mapgen2 lacks). Because the land is ranked, about 16% of it always ends up below 0.16 moisture, the dry end of the biome table, so sources only decide *where* the driest land is. With fresh water alone, the driest land was the coastal strip between river mouths, which turned coasts into desert. A sea strength of 1 or less doesn't fix that, because big rivers outrank the coast for about 110 px. At 2, the deserts move to continental interiors. `Skew` (default 1) sets how arid the world is by raising each land corner's evenly spaced rank to that power, which leaves the land's order unchanged. The share of land below 0.16 is `0.16^(1/Skew)`: 16% at 1, 40% at 2 (arid, with green strips along the rivers), and 3% at 0.5 (wet). It runs a Dijkstra over land and coast corners, never ocean corners. Edge lengths are in pixels, and a corner's priority is its dryness, `distance − Spread·ln(strength)`. The heap stores each entry's priority, because a corner's dryness can drop while it is queued. Land corners (not ocean or coast) are then ranked into evenly spaced 0…1 values, with land no fresh water reaches the driest. Ocean and coast corners are 1, and a cell is the mean of its corners. Ranking means `Spread` only weighs big rivers against near ones.
6. **`biomes`** (article §7): `Classify` is mapgen2's `Map.as` table. Ocean comes first. Lakes are MARSH below altitude 0.1, ICE above 0.8, and LAKE otherwise. A land cell next to the ocean is ROCKY_SHORE when its altitude is above `RockyAltitude` (default 0.04, about half the coast on the 2k map), and BEACH otherwise. Rocky shore becomes CLIFF when its `CoastSlope` is above `CliffSlope` (default 0.01 altitude per pixel). `CoastSlope` is the steepest drop to an ocean neighbor, including the water's depth, divided by the distance between the cells' sites, so it doesn't depend on cell size. At the default, cliffs are about 8% of the 2k map's coast but 29% of the default test map's, whose 1,000-round terrain is steeper at the coast. ROCKY_SHORE and CLIFF are our additions, not mapgen2's. Other land is looked up by altitude zone (above 0.8, 0.6 or 0.3) and moisture. `Altitude` is linear from sea level (0) to the highest cell (1). mapgen2 instead ranks elevations into fixed zone shares, and we don't, so a low world has little snow: about 1.5–2% of land is above 0.8, against mapgen2's 5.6%. `Biome.String()` returns the mapgen2 name, which keys the render palette. `BorderCliffs` (flag `-border-cliffs`) turns every land cell with a corner on the map border into CLIFF, ahead of the rest of the table. It changes biomes only: rivers still drain off the edge through the wall. `Classify` is a method on `Options` that takes a `biomes.Cell`, because the coastal thresholds and `BorderCliffs` are options.

`README.md` documents the CLI for users, with pictures from `docs/`. When a render, a default or the pipeline changes, regenerate `docs/` with the four commands listed under "Renders" in the README. Keep its flags table and examples in step with `cmd/wg`, and run the examples after changing a flag. `cmd/wg` runs every stage once. Each stage option is a flag whose default comes from that stage's `DefaultOptions()`, so the flag help changes when a default does. The logic is in `run(args, stdout, stderr)`, so tests can call it. Gamemasters choose maps by number of land cells, not pixels: `-land-cells` works out the map size from `CellSize` and `OceanPercent`, keeping the width:height ratio of `-width` and `-height`, and lands within about 1% of the target. `-render` takes a comma list of `topo, mesh, terrain, rivers, moisture, biomes`, or `all` or `none`, and writes `<out>/<name>-<render>.png`. The topo render uses the mesh's ocean percent.

`heightmap.Map` stores elevations row-major (`Data[y*Width+x]`), whereas mapgen used `[x][y]`.

`render` is for tests and visual checks only:
- `Topo` follows the recipe in `valle-escondido.sh` from github.com/mdhender/vetopo: a six-stop color ramp, multidirectional hill shading rescaled to 25–100% and multiplied into the colors, minor and index contours, water tinted by depth, and a coastline. Its sea level comes from the share of *pixels*, and it blurs a copy of the heights (`Smooth`) to hide the fracture steps.
- `Mesh` colors whole cells with the same ramps. `Terrain` does the same, adds lakes (mapgen2's LAKE color) with their shores, and draws each land corner's downslope line. `Rivers` draws `Terrain` without drainage lines and adds each river at a width of `Scale·√flow` pixels (flow is an area, so `√flow` is a length). `Moisture` colors land cells from mapgen2's SUBTROPICAL_DESERT to TROPICAL_RAIN_FOREST colors, with lakes and rivers on top. `Biomes` paints each cell its biome color, with COAST and LAKESHORE outlines and rivers on top. All mapgen2 colors live in `render/palette.go`, copied from `resources/biome-colors.js` (never executed). `TestPaletteMatchesResource` parses the JS and fails if the two differ. Biomes mapgen2 lacks take their colors from `ownColors` (ROCKY_SHORE `#8a8478`, CLIFF `#6b6e6a`), and the test requires every biome to be in exactly one of the two tables (mapgen2's RIVER color).

`resources/` holds mapgen2's biome palette (`biome-colors.js`, Apache 2.0) and map icons (`map-icons.svg`, CC BY 4.0: credit Red Blob Games wherever the icons appear), for coloring biomes later.

The two renders flood different things (pixels versus cells), so their coastlines differ, and that is expected.

The pixel-center fill rule (a pixel belongs to a polygon when `left <= x+0.5 < right`) appears in both `voronoi.elevation` and `render.fillPolygon`. Keep the two in sync.
